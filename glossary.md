# Glossary

**Additive transition** — A connector that adds, clarifies, illustrates, emphasizes, or marks similarity (Ch 4).

**Adversative transition** — A connector that contrasts, concedes, distinguishes, dismisses, or offers an alternative (Ch 4).

**Causal transition** — A connector that expresses reason, condition, purpose, consequence, or result (Ch 4).

**Coherence** — Clear relationships among words, clauses, sentences, paragraphs, and the article’s main topic (Ch 4).

**Collocation** — A conventional combination of words in a language or discipline (Ch 2).

**Concluding sentence** — The sentence that synthesizes a paragraph and may prepare the next idea (Ch 3).

**Cross-section alignment** — Correspondence among aims, methods, results, interpretations, and conclusions (Ch 5).

**Deductive organization** — Presenting a controlling assertion before its supporting explanation or evidence (Ch 3).

**IMRAD** — The Introduction, Methods, Results, and Discussion structure of an empirical article (Ch 5).

**Internal logic** — The defensible sequence connecting purpose, procedure, evidence, interpretation, and conclusion (Ch 1).

**Lexical coherence** — Continuity created by wording that helps readers track concepts, such as stable keywords and clear references (Ch 2, Ch 4).

**Logical coherence** — Continuity created by orderly placement of main ideas and their supporting details (Ch 4).

**Logical flow** — Smooth, orderly progression of ideas, sentences, paragraphs, and sections toward a sound conclusion (Ch 1).

**Move-step model** — A model of section structure based on ordered rhetorical functions (Ch 5).

**Nominalization** — A noun construction, sometimes replacing a more direct verb form (Ch 2).

**One idea–one paragraph** — The principle that every paragraph should develop one controlling idea (Ch 3).

**Paragraph unity** — Relevance of all sentences to the paragraph’s controlling assertion (Ch 3).

**Parallel structure** — Use of matching grammatical forms for comparable ideas (Ch 2).

**Sequential transition** — A connector that orders stages, continues a process, resumes information, summarizes, or concludes (Ch 4).

**Supporting sentence** — A sentence that explains, defines, evidences, qualifies, or illustrates the topic sentence (Ch 3).

**Topic sentence** — A sentence that states a paragraph’s specific controlling assertion and frames what follows (Ch 3).

**Transition** — A word, phrase, sentence, or paragraph that makes the relationship between consecutive ideas explicit (Ch 4).

**Transitional paragraph** — A paragraph that orients the reader when moving into a new major section or information block (Ch 4).

**Transitional sentence** — A sentence that refers back to the previous paragraph’s topic while establishing the next topic (Ch 4).

**Unity** — The property that all parts contribute to one main idea or article purpose (Ch 1, Ch 3).
